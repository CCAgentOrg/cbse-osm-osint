Know Your Coempt

The Chary Family, S. Sadagopan, and Ventureast — How a Small Hyderabad Firm Captured India’s Exam Infrastructure

Published: 31 May 2026

In the last week of May 2026, as CBSE results for 18.5 lakh Class 12 students lay in disarray, one company became the centre of a national scandal: Coempt EduTeck Private Limited. Answer sheets were allegedly scanned with missing pages, marks blurred beyond recognition, and in some cases, scores swapped between students. The CBSE was forced to announce a re-evaluation for thousands.

The outrage was immediate. But beneath the anger lay a more structural question: how did a small, relatively unknown Hyderabad company with 5 directors and a ₹20 crore paid-up capital become the gatekeeper of India’s largest school examination system?

The answer involves three interconnected nodes: a family that has been building education-tech companies since 2000, an academic-turned-governance insider who has been advising that family’s company since 2008, and a venture capital firm whose founder shares deep institutional roots with both of them.

I. The Chary Family: Twenty-Six Years of Education Infrastructure

The Patriarch

Anantha Madabhushini Chary (DIN 00005042) is among the earliest directors ever registered in India’s MCA database — his DIN places him in the first ~55 directors in the country. Over 32+ years, he has been associated with at least 11 companies spanning education technology, medical diagnostics, pharmaceuticals, trading, and finance. His career is the spine of the Coempt story.

He founded Globarena Technologies Private Limited on 11 April 2000 (CIN U72200TG2000PTC034224). The company was registered at Hyderabad, Telangana. In 2019, Globarena rebranded to Coempt EduTeck — a name change that coincided with the Telangana Intermediate exam scandal that nearly destroyed the company.

Chary is the Managing Director and the constant presence across the company’s entire 26-year history. Every other director has come and gone. He remains.

The pattern: The Chary family operates through a constellation of companies across education, healthcare, pharma, trading, and finance. Two of the family entities (Charys Investments, Charys Finance) have been struck off by the Registrar of Companies — a flag that warrants scrutiny, though not necessarily indicative of wrongdoing. The family’s economic weight is anchored in Concept Industries (₹412 crore authorized capital) and Coempt EduTeck (₹20 crore paid-up, ₹68 crore revenue in FY2025).

II. S. Sadagopan: The Academic in the Room

Who Is He?

Professor Sowmyanarayanan Sadagopan (DIN 00118285) is one of India’s most networked academics. His CV reads like a directory of Indian technology governance:

  • IIT Kanpur faculty (1979–1995)
  • IIM Bangalore faculty (1995–1999)
  • Founder-Director, IIIT Bangalore (1999–2021) — 22 years
  • Director/governor at: IIT Madras Research Park, IDRBT (RBI’s banking tech institute), BEL (defense PSU), Bank of India, NMDC, NLC India, NTPC, IOCL, Indian Overseas Bank, IREDA, BEML, NSE, Tata Elxsi, Ajax Engineering, and others.
  • Currently at Ahmedabad University as a professor.

He has been involved in national tech initiatives including NPCI/IMPS (digital payments), MOSIP (open-source identity), e-Governance through NeGD/NESL, and education technology.

The Globarena Connection

The critical fact, confirmed by Sadagopan’s own website (profsadagopan.com/board-membership-consulting.html):

“Advisor, Globarena, (Strategic Business) (Jul 2008 onwards)”

Under a section titled “Mentoring Startups”, Sadagopan lists himself as a Strategic Business Advisor to Globarena (now Coempt) from July 2008 — with no listed end date.

This means:

  • He advised Globarena for 14 years before formally joining the board in September 2022.
  • His advisory tenure covers the 2017 Telangana TSBIE contract (₹4.3 crore) and the 2019 Telangana Intermediate exam disaster.
  • His advisory also covers the period when Globarena rebranded to Coempt and bid for the CBSE OSM contract.

The Revolving Door

Sadagopan’s career illustrates a structural problem in Indian governance: the same individuals circulate between academia, government advisory bodies, PSU boards, and private companies — often in sectors that overlap with their advisory roles.

His confirmed government-tech governance roles:

  • Additional Director, IIT Madras Research Park (past)
  • Additional Director, National E-Governance Services Limited (NESL) (past)
  • Chairman, Governing Council, IDRBT (RBI’s banking technology institute)
  • Member, Karnataka Vision Group on IT
  • Member, BITES (Karnataka IT policy body)
  • Director on 8+ PSU boards (BEL, Bank of India, NMDC, NLC, NTPC, IOCL, IOB, IREDA, BEML)

None of these roles are in examination technology specifically. But they sit within the broader government technology procurement ecosystem — where norms, vendor preferences, and evaluation criteria are shaped.

The structural concern: India has no mandatory disclosure requirements for informal advisory roles to companies holding government contracts. Whether Sadagopan did anything wrong or not, the system that allowed him to advise a company for 14 years while occupying government technology governance positions is itself a regulatory gap.

III. Ventureast: The VC Gateway

The Firm

Ventureast (Ventureast Advisors Pvt Ltd), founded by Sarath Naru in 1997, is India’s longest-standing venture capital firm. Key facts:

  • Founded: 1997
  • Assets under management: $400+ million across 6 funds
  • Portfolio: 80+ companies, including IPOs (ACKO, Zaggle, VideoVerse) and acquisitions
  • Sectors: DeepTech, Enterprise SaaS, B2B, Healthcare, Consumer
  • HQ: Bangalore (Museum Road), with Naru split across Chennai, Hyderabad, and Bangalore
  • Notable exits: 120x on VideoVerse, 50x exit to a Japanese listed company, 13.5x on Facebook’s first India acquisition

The Founder: Sarath Naru

Naru’s profile (ventureast.net/team/sarath-naru):

  • B.Tech, IIT Madras — the same institution where IIT Madras Research Park operates
  • MBA, University of Chicago Booth (1981–1983)
  • Former entrepreneur (India-USA trading business)
  • Lives across Chennai, Hyderabad, and Bangalore — the three cities that form the Chary-Coempt geographic triangle
  • Son works at Walmart in Sunnyvale, CA; daughter is a designer in New York

The Elbit Investment

Ventureast’s own website lists Elbit Medical Diagnostics as a portfolio company (ventureast.net/practice-areas/elbit). The status: “Exited to family office.”

Elbit Medical Diagnostics is the company where Chary, Aleti, and Raju — the Coempt director trio — served as directors. Ventureast invested in this Chary-family healthcare company and later exited to a “family office” — likely the Chary family itself or an affiliated entity.

This establishes a direct capital connection:

Ventureast (Sarath Naru)
    ↓ invested in
Elbit Medical Diagnostics (Chary + Aleti + Raju)
    ↓ same director trio
Coempt EduTeck (Chary + Aleti + Raju + Sadagopan + Jayaraman)

The IIT Madras Link

Naru is an IIT Madras alumnus. Sadagopan served as Additional Director on the IIT Madras Research Park board. Jayaraman (DIN 07234119) is the NRI on the Coempt board with a Tamil Nadu connection.

IIT Madras Research Park (respark.iitm.ac.in) is India’s premier deep-tech incubation ecosystem. While ZaubaCorp records confirm Sadagopan was once an Additional Director there, the current leadership page no longer lists him — suggesting his tenure ended before or around 2022.

The IIT Madras thread connects all three nodes: Naru (alumnus), Sadagopan (former board member), and the research park itself as a potential source of technology validation and credibility for companies in its orbit.

The Broader Portfolio

Ventureast’s portfolio extends across sectors — from MoEngage (marketing automation) to LOHUM (battery recycling) to ACKO (insurance). None of the other portfolio companies are in the exam-tech space. The Elbit/Chary connection appears to be Ventureast’s sole foray into the Chary family ecosystem.

What Ties These Three Nodes Together?

1. Geographic overlap: Hyderabad (Chary HQ), Chennai (Naru residence, Jayaraman TN connection), Bangalore (Ventureast HQ, Sadagopan’s IIIT-B).

2. Institutional overlap: IIT Madras (Naru alumnus, Sadagopan IITMRP board, research park credibility). IIT Kanpur/IIM-Bangalore (Sadagopan’s academic career).

3. Sectoral overlap: Ventureast invested in the Charys’ healthcare company (Elbit). The Charys run India’s examination marking infrastructure (Coempt). Sadagopan bridges academia, government tech governance, and the Charys’ company.

4. Temporal overlap: Sadagopan advised Globarena from 2008. Jayaraman joined the Coempt board in September 2022 — the same month as Sadagopan. The CBSE contract followed.

V. What This Means

The Structural Problem

India’s examination technology market — worth thousands of crores across CBSE, NTA, SSC, and state boards — is served by a handful of small, opaque companies with interlocking directorships, academic advisors, and VC connections.

Coempt EduTeck is not an anomaly. It is a case study in how this market works:

  • Small companies with limited accountability capture massive government contracts
  • Academic advisors provide institutional credibility without mandatory disclosure
  • Revolving doors between government boards and private companies go untracked
  • Tender processes get re-engineered when large players (TCS) outbid small firms
  • Cross-party political connections (BRS in Telangana, BJP at the Centre, AIADMK in Tamil Nadu) insulate companies from political risk

What We Know for Certain

  1. Coempt EduTeck is a Chary family-controlled company operating through 11+ interlocking entities since 2000.
  2. S. Sadagopan advised the company for 14 years (2008–2022) before joining the board, per his own website.
  3. Ventureast invested in the Charys’ healthcare company (Elbit Medical Diagnostics) and exited to a “family office.”
  4. Jayaraman holds concurrent positions at Coempt and a Tamil Nadu government PSU (TNSTDC).
  5. The CBSE OSM tender was re-engineered after initial failures — conditions were relaxed, and Coempt won.
  6. Coempt has faced major exam bungling before — the 2019 Telangana Intermediate results disaster.

What We Don’t Know — And Should

  • Whether Sadagopan’s advisory was compensated, and whether it created any fiduciary obligation
  • Whether Ventureast’s Elbit exit proceeds flowed to the Chary family or other entities
  • Whether any of the three nodes (Chary, Sadagopan, Ventureast) communicated about the CBSE tender process
  • Why Jayaraman and Sadagopan joined the Coempt board in the same month (September 2022)
  • Whether Coempt’s contracts in “opposition-ruled states” (as BJP defenders claim) were awarded through comparable tender processes

The Reform Question

The CBSE OSM scandal is not primarily about Coempt. It is about a system that allows opaque companies to become the nervous system of public examinations — with no transparency on advisory relationships, no cooling-off periods between government service and private board seats, and no mandatory disclosure of financial ties between academic advisors and exam-tech vendors.

India moved 18.5 lakh answer sheets through a company controlled by a family with a record of exam bungling, advised by an academic with government governance roles, connected to a VC firm with institutional ties to the ecosystem.

That the system permitted this is the scandal. The scan errors and blurred marks are just the symptoms.
